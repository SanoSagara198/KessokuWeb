## 1. Studio Positioning

### Core promise

**Production-grade Roblox worlds with lasting consequences.**

This should be the central studio-level promise across the homepage and shared brand language.

### Primary audience

**Competitive Roblox players.**

The website should prioritize clarity, depth, mastery, progression, tactical decision-making, and credible systems over broad casual messaging.

### Primary visitor action

**Play the games.**

The website's primary calls to action should direct visitors toward playing Super Soldiers and Heist City. Secondary actions such as reading development updates, exploring systems, or viewing media should support that goal rather than compete with it.

## 2. Current Design Implications

- Lead with the games rather than the studio biography.
- Present each game as a complete competitive or systemic experience, not as a technical demonstration.
- Use concise hero copy followed by deeper feature sections.
- Make playable value clear before discussing architecture.
- Use strong, repeated play calls to action at the hero, game-detail, and closing sections.
- Describe technical systems only through their effect on the player.
- Avoid vague claims such as “immersive” unless tied to a concrete mechanic or consequence.
- Distinguish implemented features, active development, and future direction.

## 4. Super Soldiers — Core Product Definition

### Core fantasy

Super Soldiers combines four fantasies at once:

1. **Empowered soldier** — every operator enters combat with one or more defining passive or active capabilities that can materially alter a match.
2. **Competitive arena fighter** — players win through movement, execution, positioning, timing, and adaptation.
3. **Tactical squad member** — individual skill matters, but coordinated team logic and composition can overturn an equipment disadvantage.
4. **Persistent operator owner** — time spent playing produces resources that improve operators and weapons without invalidating the player's earlier choices.

The name “Super Soldiers” is literal in gameplay terms. Operators are not ordinary soldiers with cosmetic differences. Each is intentionally overpowered in a distinct, balanced way.

### Competitive format

The primary mode is an intense **5v5 PvP match**.

Every player may prepare up to four loadouts before entering the battlefield. Only one loadout is active at a time, but the player may choose again at match start and after death when respawning.

This is a central strategic feature. A player is not permanently trapped in a loadout that has become ineffective. They can respond to:

- the enemy team's current composition;
- the evolving state of the match;
- a tactical problem that the current loadout cannot solve;
- a particular opponent or ability;
- whether the team currently needs pressure, control, survival, disruption, or another function.

The four-loadout structure turns death and respawn into a tactical adaptation point rather than only a punishment.

### Progression and resources

Playing produces resources through multiple reward channels, with normal play as the primary source.

Those resources are used to improve:

- operators;
- weapons;
- potentially the equipment and capabilities associated with a loadout.

Progression should create a meaningful advantage and a reason to continue improving equipment. However, equipment level must not replace player skill.

A highly upgraded player has an advantage, but should still lose to superior:

- movement;
- positioning;
- target selection;
- timing;
- tactical adaptation;
- squad coordination.

The desired relationship is:

> Progression increases potential. Player skill and team logic determine how much of that potential becomes a win.

### Fair competitive authority

Competitive fairness is a defining product promise.

The previous client-authority implementation could only approximate a fair competitive result and could produce outcomes that felt inconsistent or unjust. The server-authoritative runtime exists to provide the credible shared truth expected from a serious competitive game.

The website should communicate the player-facing result rather than implementation detail:

- the same hit, damage, death, score, and match outcome for everyone;
- responsive play without client-decided combat truth;
- no player receiving a privileged version of the battle;
- competition that feels dependable enough to master seriously.

### Mobile-first competitive parity

Super Soldiers is intended to be playable anywhere, especially on mobile, without becoming a lesser version of the game.

The core promise is not merely “mobile supported.” It is:

> The same tactical depth, combat truth, progression, and competitive fairness wherever the player enters the match.

Controls and presentation may adapt to device, but the game rules and competitive legitimacy do not.

### Combat identity

The match should support several rhythms simultaneously:

- fast mechanical engagements;
- deliberate positioning;
- explosive ability moments;
- periods of regrouping and recovery;
- tactical counter-adjustment through loadout selection;
- high-pressure overtime and comeback situations.

Different operators and equipment may create different local rhythms, but all remain part of the same intense match.

### Operator and equipment balance doctrine

Each operator has a relative combat role, but roles are not a hierarchy.

No operator should exist as the objectively weaker beginner option or as a disposable stepping stone toward a superior character. Each operator is intended to be equally powerful and useful within its correct tactical context.

The same doctrine applies to equipment:

- every item should have a legitimate use;
- every item should reward an appropriate strategy;
- players should be able to choose what feels natural to them;
- victory should not require copying a single dominant build;
- the game should resist a stale “use the meta or lose” environment.

The desired message is:

> Choose the operator and equipment that fit how you think, learn their correct use, and win through mastery.

### Operator permanence

Players may naturally become attached to a small initial set of operators and invest in those first.

That investment must remain valid permanently. An operator chosen early should continue to be useful at high-level play. The product should not routinely invalidate player investment through destructive nerfs, deliberate obsolescence, or replacement by newly released superior operators.

Balance changes may be necessary, but the product promise is that an operator's core value and identity will not be downgraded into irrelevance.

### Counterplay philosophy

The central balancing idea is not that every powerful action requires a standardized warning or direct hard counter.

Instead:

- each operator and equipment piece has a correct use;
- each has situations where it is highly effective;
- tactical judgment decides whether that power is applied productively;
- positioning, timing, composition, and adaptation are the real counters.

The game may contain overwhelming moments, provided they emerge from correct preparation and execution rather than from an unavoidable dominant item.

### Match memory

A successful match should generate several kinds of memorable moments:

- a perfectly coordinated push;
- a near-death duel won through execution;
- an overtime hold;
- a loadout change that reverses the match;
- a favorite operator proving permanently valuable;
- a squad overcoming better-upgraded opponents;
- an unexpected bot or teammate intervention;
- a dramatic combination of abilities.

### Tone and fiction

The desired tone blends:

- serious military conflict;
- stylized competitive sport;
- superhero-scale empowered combat;
- science-fiction warfare.

The website should lead with gameplay clarity, while gradually introducing the world, conflict, operators, technology, and fiction as deeper layers.

### Accessibility and rank separation

The basic interaction should be understandable quickly, while mastery develops through:

- movement;
- operator knowledge;
- equipment knowledge;
- loadout adaptation;
- tactical positioning;
- squad coordination.

Rank-separated matchmaking is an important part of accessibility and competitive integrity. New players should primarily meet players at a comparable stage, while experienced competitors face opponents capable of testing the deeper systems.

### Working website statement

> Super Soldiers is a mobile-first 5v5 tactical action game where empowered operators bring match-changing abilities into a shared, server-authoritative battlefield. Build up operators and weapons through play, prepare four adaptable loadouts, and change your tactical answer each time you deploy. Equipment expands your options, but movement, judgment, and squad coordination decide the match.

### Product principles extracted from the interview

- Competitive truth must be shared and credible.
- Mobile is a first-class competitive platform.
- Progression should motivate play without replacing skill.
- Every operator must remain valuable.
- Every equipment choice should have a legitimate tactical purpose.
- Loadout adaptation is part of the match, not only pre-match preparation.
- Different playstyles should remain viable.
- Strong individual execution and strong teamwork must both matter.
- Fiction should enrich the product without obscuring the gameplay promise.

## 5. Super Soldiers — Match, Progression, Economy, and Competitive Structure

### 5.1 Definitive PvP mode catalogue

Super Soldiers has three intended 5v5 PvP formats. Each uses a four-minute regulation window, infinite respawns, persistent per-player kill/assist/death statistics, and an objective-specific overtime ratification period.

#### Mode A — The Bag

A physical bag of stolen money appears at the center of the battlefield 30 seconds after the match begins.

Core rules:

- Any player or bot may collect it by touching it.
- The bag is visibly equipped on the carrier's back.
- The bag's current world location is always exposed in every player's interface.
- The carrier can move, shoot, swap weapons, and use special equipment normally.
- Carrying the bag imposes no movement, combat, cooldown, or equipment penalty; permanent exposure is the tradeoff.
- Killing the carrier drops the same bag into the battlefield indefinitely until another combatant collects it.
- Every kill still grants 2 team points.
- A carrier's team receives 4 additional points after that individual carrier completes 10 uninterrupted seconds of possession.
- The possession interval belongs to the individual carrier, not merely the team.
- Any carrier change resets the 10-second interval, including an immediate handoff to a teammate.
- The score target is 100 points, and reaching it may end regulation early subject to the mode's victory ratification behavior.

Recovery policy:

- If the bag becomes inaccessible or leaves the legal arena, a 10-second recovery countdown begins.
- The bag then respawns at its valid authored location.
- While the bag is absent after having entered play, no team counts as possessing it.
- Therefore, an overtime continuation based on the losing team holding the bag ceases if the bag becomes inaccessible or leaves the arena.

Regulation and overtime:

- Regulation lasts four minutes.
- If the lower-scoring team holds the bag when regulation expires, the match continues into overtime.
- Overtime does not produce an immediate victory the moment a score condition changes.
- When either team satisfies the current victory condition, a 10-second ratification countdown begins.
- The condition must remain valid throughout that countdown.
- A tie, a lead reversal, carrier death, bag loss, or invalid bag state can cancel or reset the countdown according to which victory condition was broken.
- A trailing team can therefore recover the bag, take the lead, and then preserve the valid winning condition for 10 seconds.
- The currently higher-scoring team can win when the opposing team's bag-possession continuation condition becomes false and the higher-score condition remains valid for the ratification period.

Player fantasy:

The objective is not an abstract token. It is a bag of money from a heist being fought over in real time. Its carrier becomes the moving center of the battle and is permanently exposed to every player.

#### Mode B — Ground Control *(working name)*

The battlefield contains two possible control zones, Site A and Site B. Each zone is approximately 2 studs in radius, subject to final map readability and collision tuning.

Initial capture:

- Attackers and defenders are assigned once when the match starts.
- Either site may be attacked initially.
- One uncontested attacker requires 10 seconds of accumulated capture progress.
- Additional net attackers accelerate the capture rate by approximately 20% each, up to an intended 80% rate increase with full-team commitment.
- The exact formula remains a tuning detail, but the intended full-team capture time is approximately 5.5 to 6 seconds.
- Opposing occupants cancel one another numerically.
- Example: four attackers and three defenders count as one net attacker.
- Equal effective presence produces no progress.
- A single opponent is enough to pause an otherwise single-player capture.
- If attackers leave before completion, stored progress begins to regress rather than disappearing instantly.
- Attackers may return and resume from the remaining progress.

Active-zone control:

- Once attackers complete capture on one site, the other site is disabled.
- The active site begins a continuous 72-second control countdown.
- Attackers do not need to remain inside continuously after establishing control.
- The countdown continues whenever no effective defending presence contests the zone.
- Defender presence pauses or reverses the control contest according to net occupancy.
- Defenders must complete a recapture process using the same 10-second base capture logic and multi-player acceleration.
- Defender recapture must remain uninterrupted by superior attacking presence.
- A successful defender recapture resets the entire 72-second attacking objective progress.
- After recapture, both Site A and Site B become available again.

Regulation and overtime:

- Regulation lasts four minutes.
- The mode has no 100-point team target.
- Kills, assists, and deaths remain recorded for player statistics.
- If regulation expires while attackers retain control of an active site, overtime continues.
- Overtime persists until either:
  - the 72-second attacking control countdown reaches zero, producing an attacking victory; or
  - defenders fully recapture the active site, producing a defending victory.
- The match is one round; teams do not swap attacking and defending sides during that match.

The working public name is **Ground Control**, emphasizing possession of a tactical zone rather than a conventional bomb or capture-point metaphor.

#### Mode C — Team Battle

Team Battle is the direct score-combat format with infinite respawns.

Core rules:

- Every opponent death grants the other team 2 points.
- This includes weapon kills, environmental deaths, and self-inflicted deaths.
- Assists are recorded but do not grant team points.
- Regulation lasts four minutes.
- The score target is 100 points.
- Reaching 100 may end the match before regulation expires.

Overtime:

- A tied regulation enters overtime.
- When one team gains the lead, a 10-second victory countdown begins.
- The leading team must preserve the lead for the complete countdown.
- Returning to a tie resets the countdown.
- A later lead creates a new countdown for the newly leading team.

Team Battle is the purest expression of movement, fighting, target selection, loadout adaptation, and squad pressure.

### 5.2 Match-stat philosophy

Every PvP format records:

- kills;
- assists;
- deaths.

These statistics describe performance and remain part of the player's career record, but they should not independently determine progression payout. Assists do not add team score in Team Battle or The Bag. The reward model should avoid encouraging stat protection, kill stealing, or objective abandonment.

### 5.3 Death and respawn

Death has two direct consequences:

1. The player is removed from the battle for the fixed respawn delay.
2. The opposing team receives 2 points in score-bearing modes, including when the death is environmental or self-inflicted.

There is no inventory loss, resource loss, limited-life system, escalating respawn punishment, or permanent match penalty. Respawns are infinite.

The respawn interval is also a tactical decision window in which the player may select another of their four prepared loadouts. If no new choice is made, the previous loadout is used automatically.

### 5.4 Four-loadout adaptation system

Each player prepares up to four loadouts in MainMenu before entering matchmaking.

Rules:

- Loadouts cannot be edited inside a match.
- Each loadout must use a different operator.
- The same operator cannot appear in two of the player's prepared loadouts.
- Selecting another loadout therefore always means changing operator.
- A loadout may be selected at initial deployment and during the death/respawn countdown.
- If the player makes no new selection, the previous loadout is selected automatically.
- Players cannot inspect enemy loadouts during selection.
- Players cannot inspect teammate loadouts during selection.
- Loadout choice is private to the player.

This means adaptation is based on observed battlefield behavior rather than perfect composition information.

The player must read:

- which enemy capabilities are affecting the match;
- where the team is losing control;
- whether the objective requires pursuit, survival, area denial, range, mobility, or another resource;
- which of their four prepared operators is the best answer.

### 5.5 Loadout composition

The standard loadout contains:

- one operator;
- one main weapon;
- one secondary weapon;
- two special-equipment slots.

A specific operator may possess a third special-equipment slot as part of that operator's identity.

Appearance is not loadout-specific:

- operator appearance belongs to the operator;
- weapon appearance belongs to the weapon;
- switching loadout does not create a separate cosmetic variant of the same operator or item.

### 5.6 Operator kit doctrine

Operators do not carry conventional active ability kits.

Instead, every operator has one or more permanent built-in passive traits. Examples of the intended design space include:

- life leech;
- resistance to a damage or elemental category;
- improved evasion;
- other passive changes to how the operator survives, attacks, moves, recovers, or uses equipment.

Operators may have broad relative roles and subtypes, but the game should not impose rigid role labels on the player. Identity should be discovered through play.

An operator defines the foundation of the entire loadout, while weapons and special equipment provide the player's active tactical tools.

### 5.7 Operator upgrades and talents

Operator progression extends to approximately 100 levels.

Intended structure:

- Base operator upgrades may increase health and improve built-in passive traits.
- Every 10 operator levels unlocks a talent tier.
- A tier may offer up to three talents.
- The player chooses one talent from the available options for that tier.
- Talent choice is not permanent.
- Players may change selected talents outside the match.
- Talents refine an operator's tactical interpretation without replacing its core identity.

The principal value of progression is not intended to be a simple percentage increase. Progression adds options, passive refinements, talent combinations, and greater survivability.

A low-level operator can still defeat a highly upgraded equivalent through superior resource use, weapon selection, movement, timing, and tactics. Certain weapon categories—such as high-impact explosive tools—may overcome the additional health granted by operator progression.

### 5.8 Weapon doctrine

Weapons are unrestricted by operator. Any operator may equip any owned compatible weapon.

Weapons should differ through a broad mechanical vocabulary, including:

- damage;
- effective range;
- rate of fire;
- accuracy;
- reload commitment;
- ammunition model;
- area control;
- movement implications;
- damage category;
- status effects;
- tactical utility;
- interaction with traps, buffs, penalties, and enemy positioning.

Weapon upgrades are intended to provide a direct but restrained advantage, primarily through damage. Future milestone upgrades may also affect range, accuracy, or other authored characteristics.

The upgraded version of a weapon is intentionally stronger than its base form. At professional skill levels, a small damage advantage may decide an otherwise equal confrontation.

However, different weapon families should not collapse into one universal ranking. A weapon's correct purpose, delivery method, range, status effect, and tactical context should remain more important than raw damage alone.

### 5.9 Special-equipment doctrine

Special equipment is the primary active-resource layer of a loadout.

The design space includes:

- grenades and other explosives;
- traps;
- mines;
- deployable devices;
- shields or defensive tools;
- healing and regeneration resources;
- temporary buffs;
- enemy penalties and debuffs;
- elemental or status-effect tools;
- crowd-control resources;
- detection or information tools;
- mobility aids;
- area denial;
- anti-equipment effects;
- tools that alter objective control.

Specials may be limited through:

- cooldowns;
- charges;
- range;
- placement rules;
- duration;
- area of effect;
- vulnerability or commitment during use.

The target is an MMORPG-like breadth of damage types, penalties, buffs, and utility, translated into a readable high-intensity tactical action format.

Teamwork should emerge from positioning, timing, complementary resources, and shared tactical intent rather than from mandatory scripted ability-combo chains.

### 5.10 Permanent-value and anti-nerf doctrine

The balance promise is strict:

- A player may invest deeply in the operator, weapon, or tactical style they prefer.
- That investment should never be invalidated because it became popular or powerful.
- The preferred balancing response is to improve other operators, weapons, and resources.
- New operators should expand the set of viable answers and help rebalance the environment.
- New content should be an alternative, not a replacement tier.
- The original operator should remain competitively useful as the roster grows.

Public-facing promise:

> Your first operator is not a temporary beginner character. Mastery and investment remain valuable as the roster grows.

Seasons may reset competitive rank, but operator and equipment progression remains permanent.

There is no intended prestige reset, progression wipe, or forced refund. A future player market may allow owned progression-bearing assets or resources to be sold to other players, but the exact market model remains undefined.

### 5.11 Progression duration

Super Soldiers is designed as a long-term mastery game.

- Reaching maximum operator progression is expected to represent roughly 600 hours of play.
- Early upgrades should not be instant.
- High playtime is the main answer to the long progression path.
- Everything affecting gameplay should remain obtainable through free play.
- Losing still advances the account's general progression.
- A loss primarily affects competitive rank rather than erasing time or rewards.

This design makes persistence meaningful while avoiding a progression dead end after a few sessions.

### 5.12 Resources and reward economy

Current resource vocabulary:

- General currency — used across most upgrade and economy functions.
- Keys — used specifically to open reward chests.
- Operator pieces — jigsaw-like operator-specific resources required for operator upgrades.
- Experience and seasonal resources — possible supporting progression layers.

Potential reward sources include:

- completing matches;
- winning matches;
- seasonal participation;
- daily and weekly missions;
- clan missions;
- operator mastery;
- achievements;
- tutorials;
- login or event rewards;
- reward chests;
- optional rewarded advertising.

Personal kills, assists, or damage should not create disproportionate individual payout. Reward design should avoid encouraging selfish stat protection, kill stealing, or hostility toward weaker teammates.

### 5.13 Monetization doctrine

There should be no gameplay paywall.

Players may purchase general currency and operator pieces, but every gameplay-affecting resource, operator, weapon, special, and upgrade must also be obtainable through play.

The intended model is:

- payment may accelerate resource acquisition;
- payment must not unlock an exclusive combat capability;
- free players must be able to reach the same final gameplay state;
- time and mastery remain meaningful;
- the website should avoid claiming “cosmetic-only monetization,” because upgrade resources may be purchased;
- the website may accurately state that all gameplay content is earnable without payment.

### 5.14 Mobile-first competitive design

Super Soldiers is designed primarily around mobile accessibility rather than adapted to mobile after a desktop game was completed.

Mobile-oriented foundations include:

- elevated top-down camera;
- simplified aiming;
- automatic shooting;
- a small number of high-value controls;
- special equipment that creates tactical variety without requiring a large keyboard layout;
- the same server-authoritative match truth across devices.

The intended website statement is:

> Designed for serious competition on mobile, and built so every supported platform can enter the same tactical contest.

The game has esports ambitions, but those ambitions depend on fairness:

- server-authoritative outcomes;
- rank separation;
- device-conscious controls;
- short readable matches;
- private prepared loadouts;
- deep movement and timing mastery;
- community tournament support.

Cross-platform matchmaking is currently intended. Platform-separated queues may become a future option.

### 5.15 Rank, progression, and matchmaking

Account progression and competitive rank are separate.

Account progression measures:

- time and investment;
- operator and weapon development;
- unlocked resources;
- long-term collection.

Competitive rank measures:

- match wins and losses;
- current competitive performance.

Current matchmaking inputs:

- visible competitive rank;
- potentially an additional calculation based on unlocked equipment level.

Squad-aware matchmaking should attempt to create structurally comparable teams.

Examples:

- a four-player squad plus one player;
- versus another four-player squad plus one;
- or versus smaller groups whose combined premade structure is reasonably equivalent.

The system should favor matches containing the greatest feasible number of real players while preserving team size and squad fairness.

### 5.16 Bot continuity model

Bots serve two functions:

1. Fill the 5v5 roster when there are not enough players.
2. Take control when a human disconnects unexpectedly.

Rules:

- A bot declared in the initial roster remains a bot for the full match.
- A human does not join mid-match to replace an original filler bot.
- When a human player leaves, a bot inherits control of that player's battlefield slot.
- If the disconnected player returns, they regain control from that substitute bot.
- Bot labels should eventually be removed from public presentation.
- Players should experience bots as ordinary combatants rather than visibly separate filler entities.
- Bot operator level, weapon level, and intelligence should scale according to the opposing team's level and competitive context.

### 5.17 Definition of high-level mastery

A professional-level Super Soldiers player is distinguished by:

- superior movement;
- precise tactical timing;
- strong spatial judgment;
- the ability to create and execute strategies quickly;
- mastery of a small, deliberate set of tools rather than superficial familiarity with everything;
- understanding how four prepared loadouts cover different match situations;
- knowing when a loadout change is more valuable than repeating the previous approach;
- coordinating with squad behavior without depending on rigid ability combos;
- applying upgraded resources efficiently rather than relying on their raw level.

### 5.18 Community tournaments

Community members, clans, and streamers should be encouraged to organize custom tournaments.

Tournament configuration may eventually support optional restrictions such as:

- operator uniqueness;
- duplicate-operator limits;
- equipment restrictions;
- map pools;
- mode rotation;
- squad composition rules;
- progression normalization;
- platform restrictions;
- spectator or broadcast presentation.

These restrictions are not intended as universal matchmaking rules. They are optional competition formats for organized events.

### 5.19 Market and platform evidence for the website

Mobile-first positioning is commercially and strategically credible.

Current external evidence indicates:

- Mobile remained the largest global gaming platform in 2025.
- Mobile generated approximately $113.3 billion of the revised $201.6 billion global games market.
- An earlier 2025 market estimate placed the mobile audience at approximately 3.0 billion players, around 83% of global players.
- Roblox reported 127 million average daily active users and 123.9 billion hours of engagement in 2025 across mobile, desktop, and console.
- Roblox's 2025 filing describes mobile use as significant; 29% of revenue came through Apple's App Store and 15% through Google Play.
- Roblox explicitly designs its client and platform to adapt controls, rendering, and content delivery across low-end mobile devices, computers, and consoles.
- Roblox supports Rewarded Video as an immersive advertising format.

The website should use only a small number of these figures. The point is not to present an investor report; it is to establish that a serious mobile-first competitive game addresses the largest global gaming audience rather than a secondary platform.

### 5.20 Working long-form website interpretation

> Super Soldiers is a mobile-first 5v5 tactical action game built around three four-minute competitive formats, persistent operator mastery, and mid-match adaptation. Prepare four different operators before matchmaking, deploy one, and change your answer during the respawn window as the battlefield evolves. Operators bring permanent passive identities; weapons and special equipment supply the active tools. Progression expands health, damage, talents, and tactical options, but movement, timing, objective judgment, and squad logic remain capable of defeating a stronger account.
>
> The Bag turns one carrier into the moving center of the battle. Ground Control forces teams to commit to one of two tactical zones and preserve a continuous objective hold. Team Battle rewards direct combat under a 100-point target. Each mode resolves overtime according to its own objective rather than ending a meaningful contest at an arbitrary timer.
>
> Every operator and weapon is intended to remain valuable. Instead of invalidating a favorite build through destructive nerfs, Super Soldiers expands the roster of viable answers. New operators and resources rebalance the tactical environment by adding alternatives. All gameplay content remains obtainable through play, while the same server-authoritative combat truth applies across mobile, desktop, and controller play.
